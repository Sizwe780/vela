# Organic chemistry content extension
Version: 1.0.0

Applies only to modules explicitly registered with the `organic-chemistry` domain.

## Scope and authority
- Treat the current UNISA Tutorial Letter 101 and official myModules guide as the authority for examinable scope.
- CHE1502 is an organic-focused module. Do not import kinetics, aqueous acid-base equilibria or thermochemistry merely because those topics appear in a general-chemistry textbook or older guide.
- Record each unit's current guide outcome, source edition and source page before publication.
- Hybridization, nomenclature, stereochemistry and reaction mechanisms must distinguish structural evidence, nomenclature rules and proposed mechanisms.

## Molecular representations
- Use explicit structures, stereochemical wedges/dashes and clear reaction conditions.
- Do not imply that a 2D line formula establishes a 3D conformation or that a mechanism is an observed trajectory unless the evidence states that.
- State when curved arrows represent electron-pair movement and preserve atom, charge and electron accounting.

## Reaction families
- Confirm current outcomes for hydrocarbons (including alkanes, alkenes and alkynes), assigned derivatives, alcohols, ethers, amines and the carbonyl families listed by the module. Apply relevant representations and methods to each mapped unit.
- Distinguish addition, substitution, elimination and rearrangement as descriptive reaction classes.
- Do not publish a single “correct” mechanism as fact when conditions, substitution pattern or solvent change the route.
- Preserve stereochemical outcomes and mention relevant assumptions; do not award mastery for naming alone.

## Assessment
- Recognition: identify a functional group or stereochemical relationship.
- Procedure: choose an IUPAC name, classify a reaction or complete a bounded mechanism.
- Error correction: diagnose charge, valence, arrow direction or stereochemical inconsistency.
- Transfer: apply the rule to a changed but equivalent organic structure.

## Visuals
Molecular geometry and reaction models must be reviewed for formula, bond, charge, stereochemistry and condition accuracy. A 2D or 3D model illustrates a declared structure and does not establish a mechanism.
