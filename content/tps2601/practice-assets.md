# TPS2601 synthetic practice assets

All cases and responses below are fictional. Use them to rehearse professional reasoning. Keep them out of formal placement submissions.

## Classroom observation cards

| Time | Observable event |
| --- | --- |
| 08:12 | The teacher asks the class to decide which of two equal-size paper strips shows the greater fraction, then waits two seconds before selecting a hand. |
| 08:13 | The respondent says that the strip divided into more pieces shows the greater fraction. Three groups change their selected answer after hearing the response. |
| 08:15 | The teacher asks pairs to mark equal lengths before explaining the size of each piece. Two pairs compare the drawings at different scales. |

**Blank record** — Focus question · time and observable action · tentative interpretation · alternative explanation · next evidence to collect.

**Worked observation** — At 08:13 the respondent uses number of pieces as the explanation. This suggests attention to the denominator without relating it to piece size. They might also be comparing unequal drawings. Ask for a new comparison with clearly aligned strips and collect individual explanations before a class vote.

## Lesson rehearsal sheet

**Blank sheet** — Intended learning · opening question · demonstration or activity · participation support · misconception to watch for · evidence check · response if the check reveals a gap.

**Worked excerpt** — Learners should explain why one fourth is greater than one sixth when the wholes match. Provide two same-size strips with tactile boundaries and a spoken description. Ask every learner to indicate the greater fraction and give a reason. Where answers refer only to number of parts, align the wholes again and compare one part directly.

## Evidence and feedback cards

- **Card A** — “One sixth is bigger because six is more than four.”
- **Card B** — “One fourth is bigger because the same whole is cut into fewer equal parts.”
- **Card C** — A learner circles one fourth and leaves the explanation blank.

| Criterion | Developing | Demonstrated in rehearsal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence | Gives a broad impression | Identifies a specific response or action |
| Inference | Claims an intention or outcome from limited evidence | Relates the conclusion to evidence and names an alternative |
| Follow-up | Repeats the instruction without a diagnostic check | Proposes a feasible check that could distinguish interpretations |

**Feedback model** — Card C shows the choice and leaves the reasoning open. Ask the learner to show one fourth and one sixth on equal strips, then listen for the comparison of piece sizes.

## Portfolio reflection and evidence index

**Blank entry** — Evidence label · skill practised · observation · interpretation and limitation · next action · what evidence would show improvement.

**Worked entry** — Synthetic card set 01 records one explicit denominator misconception and one correct unexplained choice. A whole-class vote cannot show whose reasoning changed. In the next rehearsal, collect an individual explanation on an aligned strip before and after pair discussion. Compare reasoning rather than counting correct circles alone.

For a mentor conversation, bring the rehearsal plan and ask which follow-up evidence would be feasible in the classroom. Keep practice assets free of actual names, recordings, attendance and school records.

**Privacy check** — Could dates, locations, codes or quoted remarks identify a real learner or school? Does the entry distinguish an observation from an inference? Would the selected example still teach the concept with fully synthetic evidence? Replace any identifiable details before sharing a practice asset.
